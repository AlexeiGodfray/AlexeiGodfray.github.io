# A GUIDE TO LEARNING DEEP LEARNING

## "What I cannot create, I do not understand"

Richard Feynman

*Note: There are a few preferred assumptions that I have before you dive into deep learning, though if those assumptions are not true, I've outlined the steps you should take to reach them:*

1. You're pretty okay at python, though you don't have to be a wizard. If you're new to programming or a little rusty, then here are two resources you should watch in order.
  - [Neetcode python for beginners](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s3KhqPjBPaQ&t=46s)
  - [Python Object Oriented Programming](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IbMDCwVm63M&t=61s)
2. You have done AT LEAST calculus 2 (applications of integrals, intro to differential equations, techniques of integration, some analytic geometry (parametric equations, polar coordinates), infinite series).

HOWEVER, even that technically isn't really needed for just practical use. If you would like to learn the mathematics that is needed for Deep Learning at a truly in-depth level, here are a couple resources I recommend.

## Calculus 1-3:

First watch the video series below to get an idea for what Calc is

- [3b1b](https://www.3blue1brown.com/?topic=calculus)

Choose either of these two platforms for interactive learning, and practice

- [Khan Academy (free)](https://www.khanacademy.org/)
- [Ximera (free)](https://ximera.osu.edu/)

Use Paul for more practice, detailed notes and step by steps

- [Paul's (free)](https://tutorial.math.lamar.edu/Classes/CalcI/CalcI.aspx)

Use Openstax for even more practice problems, and if you like the textbook format better (like me)

- [Openstax (free)](https://openstax.org/details/books/calculus-volume-1)

This is a little redundant, but still a great set of well organized playlists that take you from 0-100 in calc 1 through 3

- [Engineering4free (in the name, free)](https://www.engineer4free.com/)

What I would do is

1. 3b1b
2. Khan Academy, and Paul at the same time
3. Engineering4free videos, if I'm truly stuck

I'm sure there are many more, but for the most part Khan Academy, Ximera, and Paul's notes will get you where you need to be. Openstax is more of a traditional textbook and engineer4free is great as well. 3b1b is amazing for building intuition, but not enough on its own. Pick one and stick with it, and if you need more practice and understanding, use other resources. If you really get into math, I'll write a roadmap for mathematics.

## HOW TO STUDY THIS?

Ironically, please don't use Claude, GPT, or other LLMs to help you out here. I know the "LLM tutor method" is useful, but make sure you have the discipline to not cheat, learn to struggle and overcome. Use a notebook and pencil.

When you start the programming applications part of everything, I recommend using a python notebook such as [jupyter](https://jupyter.org/) or [marimo](https://marimo.io/). I like to use marimo, though it's your choice.

Have fun :)
